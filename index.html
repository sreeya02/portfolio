01 · About me

A little about me.

I chose electrical engineering because of how versatile it is and how many different fields it connects to, from renewable energy and circuit design to AI and emerging technologies. What excites me most is being able to explore these different interests and apply what I learn in class to real-world projects. I especially enjoy the hands-on side of engineering, whether that's designing something from scratch, testing out ideas, or figuring out why something isn't working the way I expected.

One of my biggest interests is renewable energy and sustainability. Before coming to Georgia Tech, I got involved with Virginia Tech's Engineering & Computer Science internship program, where I've had the opportunity to work with a graduate researcher on solar technology research, write about renewable energy, and analyze electricity consumption in a building to explore ways to improve efficiency. That experience made me realize how much engineering can contribute to solving problems we see every day.

Since coming to Tech, I've also developed a growing interest in circuit and chip design. Through Silicon Jackets, I've been learning how to design and simulate circuits, work with transistor layouts, and troubleshoot designs when things don't go as planned. One of my favorite parts of engineering is finally figuring out why something isn't working after spending hours trying different solutions. It's frustrating in the moment, but getting it to work makes the whole process worth it.

I'm also really interested in the intersection of hardware, software, and AI. I've worked on projects ranging from an Arduino-based environmental monitoring system to a machine learning model that predicts water quality. I enjoy exploring different areas of engineering, and I'm still figuring out exactly where I want to take those interests.

Outside of engineering, I love dancing, meeting new people, and being involved in my community. I'm part of Jadoo, Georgia Tech's dance team, and I enjoy finding ways to stay connected with both my culture and the people around me. I've also always loved teaching, whether that was working as a math instructor at Mathnasium or leading my high school's AI Club.

I'm still at the beginning of my journey as an engineer, and there's honestly so much I want to learn. I'm excited to keep exploring new interests, challenging myself, and building things that can make a difference.

Silicon Jackets · Completed

Current-Starved Ring Oscillator

I designed and simulated a five-stage current-starved ring oscillator in the SKY130 process. I built the circuit cells, studied how the oscillation frequency changed across operating conditions, and translated the schematic into a physical layout.

Most of my learning happened while debugging. Iterating through DRC and LVS checks taught me that a layout isn't finished just because it looks connected; the physical design and the schematic have to agree exactly.

Result: Final LVS check passed with no errors. Skills: transistor-level design, simulation sweeps, layout, verification.
Cadence VirtuosoSKY130DRC / LVS
Silicon Jackets · In progress

9-Transistor Differential Amplifier

I'm currently working through a nine-transistor differential amplifier, focusing on how the input pair, biasing network, and current paths work together to amplify the difference between two signals.

This project is teaching me to think beyond "does it produce an output?" and toward "how good is that output?" I'm exploring the trade-offs between gain, bandwidth, common-mode rejection, power, and output swing.

Next: Define realistic performance specs, then simulate and evaluate. Results coming soon.

C-Tech² @ Virginia Tech · July 2025

Wild S.T.R.I.K.E.

As co-designer and project manager, I led a four-person team through the design, prototyping, and testing of an accessible wildfire-prevention kit. We developed a concentrate formula to put out campfires and built an Arduino-based monitor that tracks temperature, oxygen, and smoke levels in real time.

Managing the team taught me as much as the engineering did: splitting up work, keeping a tight schedule, and making design decisions together when we disagreed.

Result: Our formula put out campfires 82% faster than traditional methods. 3rd Place, Engineering Design Project Award, presented by Bloomberg Industries.

Poster created with Natali Long, Dishita Keswani, and Virginia Voorhees.

Regional Science Fair · 2024–2025

Predicting Water Quality with Machine Learning

Many underserved communities don't have historical water-quality monitoring data, which makes problems hard to catch early. I set out to build a low-cost prediction tool, preprocessing and visualizing regional datasets in Python and adding a time-decay factor so recent measurements counted more.

I coded and benchmarked several machine learning algorithms to find which predicted best, which taught me how much data cleaning and testing go into a model you can trust.

Result: Improved predictive accuracy by about 18%. 1st Place and $200, Regional Stockholm Junior Water Prize.

04 · Career goals

Where I want to go, and how I'll get there.

Long term, I want to work in power systems, helping design the circuits and grid technology that deliver clean, reliable energy. Solar and renewable energy are where I see myself making the biggest difference. Here's my roadmap from here to there.

Year 1 · Now

Build a strong foundation

Master the fundamentals of circuits, math, and programming. Continue my solar research, stay active in Silicon Jackets and WiECE, and land a research or internship position in power systems for Summer 2027.

Years 2–3 · Go deeper

Specialize and gain experience

Take upper-level coursework in electrical energy systems and circuit design. Join an undergraduate research lab working on power or renewable energy, and pursue internships or a co-op with a utility or energy company where I can work on real grid problems.

Year 4 · Bring it together

Own a design end to end

Lead a significant project, such as a capstone or a published research contribution, that takes an energy or circuit design from specification to tested hardware. Decide whether to go straight into industry or pursue a graduate degree first.

After graduation

Work on meaningful hardware

Join a team working on power systems, renewable energy integration, or power electronics, where I can keep learning from experienced engineers and eventually own designs from idea to implementation.
